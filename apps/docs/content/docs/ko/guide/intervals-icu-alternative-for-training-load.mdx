# Intervals.icu alternative for training load

**TL;DR:** Intervals.icu is one of the strongest free endurance analysis platforms: PMC, custom charts, interval detection, and a workout builder. Trainingload.ai is an Intervals.icu alternative when the job is **turning those signals into a confirmed weekly plan change**. Stay on Intervals.icu if chart depth and manual interpretation *are* the product.

Intervals.icu is strong for self-coached athletes who want dense charts and broad metric coverage. If your main goal is turning analysis into confirmed plan changes, Trainingload.ai is built around that loop.

## Why people look for an Intervals.icu alternative

- They already have a free PMC and still rewrite next week by hand after missed work.
- Custom charts answer "what happened" better than "what should the plan do now."
- They want an AI coach draft they can reject, without abandoning load literacy.
- They are comparing Intervals.icu with TrainingPeaks, Runalyze, or FormBeat and need a plan-linked option.

## Where Intervals.icu is especially strong

- Dense metric coverage for advanced users: power, HR, pace, custom charts, interval detection.
- Flexible trend inspection and a community that ships features from real athlete requests.
- Training calendar and workout builder on the free tier.
- Device and platform integrations (Garmin, Strava, Polar, Suunto, COROS, Wahoo, Zwift, and more).
- **Public pricing (verify on their site).** As of September 2026, Intervals.icu listed the core platform as free forever. Supporter is $4 per month for extras such as full Strava history import, custom zones, and team tools. See [intervals.icu/pricing](https://www.intervals.icu/pricing/).

There is no honest Intervals.icu alternative page that pretends those strengths are small.

## Where Trainingload.ai is different

- Keeps **plan, completed workouts, and training load** in one adjustment workflow.
- Emphasizes **plan vs actual execution** before recommending changes.
- Uses AI review as a draft step, then keeps final adjustments explicit and confirmable.
- Treats CTL / ATL / TSB and response trend (including effective VO2max) as decision context, not a dashboard to admire.
- Free calculators and converters work without an account; AI coach and adaptive plans use PLUS / PRO AI credits. See [pricing](/pricing).

Honest limits: Trainingload.ai will not match Intervals.icu chart density, custom-field flexibility, or the $0-forever analysis surface. If you love building the chart yourself, keep Intervals.icu — or run both.

## Feature comparison

| Job | Intervals.icu | Trainingload.ai |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analysis depth | Exceptional free charts and customisation | Focused load + execution views, less chart DIY |
| Training load / PMC | Free Fitness / Fatigue / Form | PMC plus plan-linked actions |
| Planning | Calendar and workout builder; annual plan on Supporter | Adaptive plan tied to completed work and AI review |
| AI coach | Not the centre of the product | Draft adjustments you confirm |
| Price to start | $0 for the core platform | Free tools; AI credits for coach / adaptive plan |

**Bottom line:** Pick Intervals.icu if your priority is chart depth and manual interpretation. Pick Trainingload.ai if your priority is plan-linked decision support and a conservative adjustment flow.

## Who should stay on Intervals.icu

Stay if you want to own the interpretation, need custom charts, or already treat Intervals.icu as the system of record for every sport file. The $4 Supporter tier is still far cheaper than most Premium analysis products if you only need extras.

## Who should use Trainingload.ai

Use Trainingload.ai if you already understand Intervals.icu charts and now want the **best training load app** behaviour to be: missed Tuesday + rising ATL → one confirmable change, not a blank Sunday night.

## Practical migration checklist

1. Keep your current threshold settings (FTP / CP / LTHR / pace) consistent.
2. Export recent FIT / GPX / TCX or use a supported connection. Convert files on the [converter](/converter) if needed.
3. Review one week with plan vs completed sessions.
4. Use load context (CTL / ATL / TSB + response trend) before changing workouts.
5. Apply one conservative adjustment and validate on the next key workout.

Overlap both products for a week. We do not invent switcher quotes or promise identical PMC numbers.
