# Lactate Threshold (LT)

In day-to-day training, many athletes treat **Lactate Threshold (LT)** as a close cousin of **[Anaerobic Threshold (AnT)](./anaerobic-threshold)**. More precisely: they often point to a similar intensity range, but the **definition and measurement method differ**, so the numbers won’t always match.

## Definition and testing

- **LT**: often determined via **blood lactate measurement** during a graded test.
  - Small samples are taken every few minutes.
  - Some systems use a fixed reference point (commonly 4 mmol/L), but that doesn’t mean everyone’s true threshold is “exactly 4”.
- **AnT**: can be determined via **gas exchange testing** or lactate breakpoints, depending on the definition used.

## Practical use

Even though terminology varies (LT, AnT, OBLA, etc.), the most useful training takeaway is:

- It’s a practical marker for the **highest steady intensity you can hold for a long time** (often described as “around an hour”, but individual variation is large).
- It’s a strong anchor for **threshold workouts** (steady intervals, tempo/threshold blocks, etc.).
- It can help set **heart-rate, pace, and power zones**, as long as you keep the testing method consistent over time.

## How Trainingload.ai uses lactate threshold

Trainingload.ai treats lactate threshold as a practical anchor, not a single magic number:

- **Zone calibration**: threshold estimates can inform heart-rate zones, pace zones, and power zones.
- **Workout review**: sessions near threshold are checked against pace/power, heart rate, RPE, and recent fatigue.
- **Trend tracking**: changes in threshold are more useful when compared with CTL, ATL, TSB, and similar recent efforts.
- **Consistency first**: a repeatable field test is often more actionable than a perfect one-off estimate.

## Why lactate matters

Lactate itself isn’t a “toxin”—it’s a glycolysis byproduct and also a fuel. When intensity rises, the processes that accompany lactate accumulation are also associated with the burning sensation and loss of force. LT is useful because it helps connect **intensity** with **sustainability**, not just suffering.
